Representing and Parsing Replacement Self-Repair in Formal Meaning Representations

Hongxu Zhou

Erasmus Mundus Joint Master Degree in LCT

Siri answers for the city the speaker took back

The speaker replaces Berlin with Dublin, but Siri returns the weather for Berlin. This is a replacement self-repair (RSR) (Shriberg 1994).

Goal: the phenomenon is real and everyday. Name it only; the anatomy opens the Related Work section.

Current voice agents make the same mistake

Full-Duplex-Bench-v3 tests six spoken agents on real disfluent speech that requires API calls (Lin et al. 2026).

The user says The system fills in The user wants
Siri Berlin, I mean, Dublin location = Berlin Dublin
Gemini Live 3.1 Rome, corrected to Milan destination = Rome Milan

On self-corrections, the best system passes 58.8% of scenarios (GPT-Realtime) and the weakest 17.6%.

Goal: the second row is the Siri error in a current agent. The paper reports this as a case (the API is called before the correction ends), not as a rate; do not claim a proportion. TODO: verify the number of self-correction scenarios (the Pass@1 values are multiples of 1/17).

This talk asks two questions

  1. RQ1: How can a meaning representation record a self-repair, keeping what was withdrawn, and what can it not cover?
  2. RQ2: How well can LLM-based parsers learn this representation from synthesised data, and what errors remain?

Related Work

An RSR has three parts

What is the weather like [in Berlin]reparandum, [I mean]editing expression, [in Dublin]repair?

  • The reparandum is the material the speaker withdraws.
  • The editing expression optionally signals the repair, e.g. I mean, actually, no.
  • The repair is the material that replaces the reparandum.

This project covers replacements within a single clause (Levelt 1983; Shriberg 1994).

NLP pipelines either delete the reparandum or tag it

Deletion

The reparandum and the editing expression are removed, and the fluent text is passed on (Johnson and Charniak 2004).

→ What is the weather like in Dublin?

Tagged transcription

The words are kept, and disfluent spans or tokens are labelled (Zayats et al. 2014; Futami et al. 2023).

→ What is the weather like in Berlin in Dublin?

Both approaches serve the same goal: recovering the speaker’s final intention.

Goal: concede that this goal is right for task-oriented pipelines that only need the final request.

Deleted or tagged, the reparandum can still matter later

What is the weather like in Berlin, I mean, in Dublin? Show me the airports of these two cities.

  • After deletion, these two cities has only one antecedent left.
  • After tagging, both names survive, but nothing records that Berlin was the location or that Dublin replaced it.

Psycholinguistic and dialogue studies show that the reparandum stays in the context (Lau and Ferreira 2005; Purver et al. 2018).

Goal: first turning point of the talk.

A system needs three pieces of information about the revision

  1. Both candidates: Berlin and Dublin
  2. Their status: Berlin is withdrawn, Dublin is accepted
  3. The role they compete for: the location in the weather request

All three concern what the utterance means, not which words were spoken.

Goal: the puzzle. Do not name the solution yet. These three items are reused as requirements (Representation); keep the wording identical.

A meaning representation is a graph of concepts and roles

What is the weather like in Dublin?

  • Nodes are concepts: weather, like, a city named Dublin.
  • Edges are roles: the city is the Location of the weather request.
  • The box on the left is a context: it holds everything the speaker asserts.

Such a graph has room for all three pieces of information: two city nodes, a context for each, and one Location role. Because the structure is explicit, an output can be checked piece by piece.

Goal: the answer to the previous slide, with a picture (Michael). Argue inspectability, not performance; the thesis does not test downstream gains. If asked where MRs are used with LLMs: Backup table. The box is introduced here so that two boxes on the Representation slide need no explanation.

SBN writes this graph as text, so an LLM can generate it

What is the weather like in Dublin?

entity.n.01 EQU ?                                   % What
time.n.08 EQU now                                   % is
weather.n.01                                        % the weather
like.a.01 Co-Theme -3 Time -2 Theme -1 Location +1  % like
city.n.01 Name "Dublin"                             % in Dublin?

Each line introduces a concept. A role points to another concept by relative position: -1 is the previous concept, +1 the next.

Simplified Box Notation (SBN) is the format of the Parallel Meaning Bank (PMB 5.1.0), which provides gold sentence–SBN pairs (Abzianidze et al. 2017; Bos 2023). Semantic parsing then becomes text-to-text: sentence in, SBN out.

Goal: the same graph as text; this is what the parser generates. Concepts are WordNet senses (weather.n.01 = first noun sense). SBN is an agreed notation; no linguistic backbone.

Pilot Study

Can an existing SBN parser handle self-repair?

  • Parser: Gemma-2 9B (Gemma Team et al. 2024), fine-tuned with LoRA (Hu et al. 2021) on fluent PMB pairs only.
  • Input: 833 PMB sentences with an inserted reparandum, with and without I mean (2,499 each).
  • Correct answer: the fluent gold SBN, i.e. the reparandum is left out (following Sen and Groves 2021).
Fluent (PMB) RSR, no editing expression RSR + I mean
A cow gave birth to a calf with two heads. A goat, a cow gave birth to a calf with two heads. A goat, I mean, a cow gave birth to a calf with two heads.

Goal: state exactly what the pilot does (Michael). The bar is deliberately low: only asks the parser to drop the reparandum, since fluent SBN cannot mark it as withdrawn. The 833 sentences come from PMB training data, so the parser had seen their fluent SBNs; only the RSR inputs were new. Reparanda: Qwen3-32B (A. Yang et al. 2025), same part of speech, before one word.

Self-repair lowers parsing accuracy, and the parser asserts the reparandum

Input Scorable (%) Failure-penalised F1
Fluent 97.5 0.950
RSR, no editing expression 94.6 0.786
RSR + I mean 76.5 0.609
  • In 90.1% of scorable RSR outputs the parse has more concepts than the fluent one: the goat is asserted, not dropped.
  • A parse that asserts the goat still scores 0.764: one graph score does not show whether the repair was handled.

Scorable: the output converts into a graph. F1: SMATCH++ (Opitz 2023), unscorable outputs counted as 0.

Goal: numbers only (Michael); the analysis is in Backup (goat/cow DAGs, recognition probe, position effects). All pairwise F1 differences significant (Wilcoxon signed-rank, p < .001). Median +2 concepts. The pilot shows the need for RSR supervision and for evaluation finer than SMATCH++; it does NOT show that SBN must be extended, that comes from later reference. The 0.764 pays off in Experiment Design.

Both cities remain available for later reference; only Dublin is the requested location

Show me the airports of these two cities.

These two cities links to both Berlin and Dublin (ANA), but Berlin does not become the requested location again.

Berlin stays accessible because proper names project out of the withdrawn context (Bos 2021). The later-reference test set only uses referents that can project in this way.

The representation covers concept and role replacement; the corpus implements single concepts

Representable Replacement of a concept or a role, also inside a context such as negation
Implemented in the corpus Replacement of a single noun, verb or adjective concept
Outside the design Replacement of a separator (e.g. must → might, when → even though); delayed repair

Goal: keep “representable” and “implemented” apart; this is where the sharpest questions will land. Why not AMR or DS-TTR: Backup.

Data

No corpus pairs self-repair with SBN, so PMB pairs are transformed on both sides

The PMB has fluent sentence–SBN pairs; disfluency corpora have self-repairs but no SBN (Deshmukh et al. 1998; Passali et al. 2022; Gupta et al. 2021).

Each item keeps the PMB split of its source document, so no training document reappears in evaluation.

Goal: why the data is generated, and how: both sides of a gold pair change together. Callback: the pilot changed only the input. The dashed “?” edge is what Experiment Design tests.

Steps ①–③: pick a concept, draw a competitor for it

Step Sentence side SBN side
① Start from a gold PMB pair What is the weather like in Dublin? the SBN shown earlier
② Pick a noun, verb or adjective that a word expresses weather weather.n.01
③ Draw a WordNet sibling: same kind of thing, different value storm storm.n.01

Other siblings of weather in the pool: cloud, sunset, aurora, halo, … Synonyms are never drawn, because the pool takes siblings only.

Goal: the audience follows one item through the pipeline; this slide and the next two are one continuous walkthrough. Step ② needs the word–concept alignment, which is why the step spans both sides. Siblings = WordNet co-hyponyms for nouns; verbs use VerbNet classes, adjectives FrameNet frames and antonyms. storm.n.01 is a real pool candidate for a PMB document with weather.n.01 (p99/d2202). Berlin → Dublin is not used here: proper names need an operator outside the implemented corpus. Q&A: “How is storm found?” → Backup #/bk-wordnet. “Isn’t a storm a kind of weather?” / “What about synonyms?” → Backup #/bk-synonyms.

Step ④: edit both sides of the pair together

What is the weather like in Dublin? → What is the storm, I mean, the weather like in Dublin?

entity.n.01 EQU ?                                    % What
time.n.08 EQU now                                    % is
entity.n.01                                          % both fill this slot
    CORRECTION <1                                    % withdrawn:
storm.n.01 EQU -1                                    %   the storm
    CONJUNCTION <2                                   % accepted (I mean):
weather.n.01 EQU -2                                  %   the weather
like.a.01 Co-Theme -5 Time -4 Theme -3 Location +1   % like
city.n.01 Name "Dublin"                              % in Dublin?

The same structure as Berlin and Dublin: a withdrawn context, an accepted context, one shared role (the Theme of like).

Goal: the pair is edited on both sides at once, so the target is known by construction; no parser is involved. Highlighted lines are what the generator adds; the indices of later concepts shift automatically. The shared entity.n.01 slot is the anchor construction, used when both candidates fill a role of a later concept (Backup: anchor and movement). The editing expression is optional: in training about 30% of items carry one. The SBN is the generator’s output for this sentence; the sentence side is shown with “the” repeated, which is the natural form.

Step ⑤: keep only pairs that are well formed and make sense

Check Rejects Rejected example
Structure ill-formed graph, wrong status, a missing link, any change outside the repair —
Contextual fit: LM surprisal of the reparandum given its left context a reparandum nobody would say cake → carburettor, for his wedding

The contextual-fit threshold is set on 150 hand-labelled candidates and frozen before the corpus is filtered.

Goal: why the corpus is not just random substitution. Status: the structural check is done over the whole training partition; contextual fit is being calibrated. Do not claim storm/weather passes the final filter: the threshold is not frozen yet. Synonyms are not a filter here: they are excluded when the pool is built (Step ③, Backup #/bk-synonyms). NLI scores are computed and archived but reject nothing. The RP still describes an NLI filter; if Koller asks, the calibration sample showed the pool already contains almost no synonyms (1 of 150), so a synonym filter had nothing to do.

The training partition yields 16,425 candidate repair sites

PMB training partition (9,552 documents) Count %
Nouns, verbs and adjectives in the gold SBNs 23,232 100.0
… expressed by a word in the sentence 17,886 77.0
… with a WordNet competitor 16,481 70.9
… giving a well-formed repair-aware graph 16,425 70.7

The transformation succeeds at 99.7% of the sites where a competitor exists. These sites are the candidate pool before linguistic filtering and sampling; the sampling rule is fixed before training.

Goal: known numbers only. Structural generation and validation are complete; linguistic filtering, the independent audit and final sampling remain. Most of the alignment loss is verbs without a surface word. 99.7% is the empirical side of RQ1’s coverage question. Scope: immediate replacement of aligned noun, verb and adjective concepts in written PMB contexts; the experiments test learnability within these boundaries, not coverage of spontaneous RSR. The sampling rule limits repeated use of a source document.

Experiment Design

Three training conditions: same inputs, different targets

Training input: What is the storm, I mean, the weather like in Dublin?

Condition Role Target for this input Answers
Fluent only reference: the pilot parser — (never sees RSR input) how far a parser gets without RSR training
RSR normalisation baseline: deletion the fluent SBN: weather only what keeping the reparandum buys and costs
Repair-aware this project storm withdrawn, weather accepted RQ2: can the representation be learned?

The controlled comparison is between the baseline and repair-aware conditions: they share inputs, example counts and training, and only the target differs.

Qwen3.5 at 2B, 9B and 27B (Qwen Team 2026) is fine-tuned with LoRA under all three conditions, with three seeds each (27 runs). One parsing instruction serves all conditions and does not say whether an input contains a repair.

Goal: pre-empt the CS reflex that the most vanilla setting is the baseline. Fluent only vs repair-aware changes both the examples and the targets, so it is a reference, not the controlled comparison. All conditions keep the same fluent PMB pairs. Three sizes so the target effect can be compared across model capacities. Qwen3.5 because it is the most recent family with dense checkpoints across the sizes needed. Normalisation is an end-to-end parser, not a pipeline.

A single graph score cannot tell opposite failures apart

Test input: What is the storm, I mean, the weather like in Dublin?

Output Typical of SMATCH++
storm dropped baseline 0.67
storm asserted as well reference 0.68
storm withdrawn, weather accepted repair-aware 1.00

The first two outputs are wrong in opposite ways: one loses the storm, the other asserts it. Yet their scores are almost the same, just as the pilot’s goat parse still scored 0.764.

Constructed outputs, scored against the repair-aware gold graph with the project’s evaluator.

Goal: the problem before the solution. These are hand-built outputs standing for each condition’s typical behaviour, not model predictions (storm_weather_scores.md). Exact: 0.667 / 0.680 / 1.000. A pilot-style output that also turns “I mean” into an event scores 0.557; not shown, the goat/cow DAGs in Backup cover that case.

Two measures tell them apart

Output SMATCH++ RSR-structure accuracy Accepted meaning
storm dropped 0.67 ✗ 1.00
storm asserted as well 0.68 ✗ 0.95
storm withdrawn, weather accepted 1.00 ✓ 1.00
  • RSR-structure accuracy (primary): both candidates, correct status, the shared role. → Can the representation be learned?
  • Accepted meaning: remove the withdrawn part, compare with the fluent graph. → Does keeping the reparandum cost the final request?
  • Later reference (Berlin/Dublin test set): can these two cities reach both? → What does keeping it buy?

Goal: each measure answers one part of RQ2. RSR-structure accuracy is read in absolute terms: only the repair-aware parser is trained on the structure, so it is expected to lead. Accepted meaning compares baseline and repair-aware; its job is the cost, not catching the reference parser; if asked about the 0.95, that is why it cannot be the primary measure. Also reported: SMATCH++ and the spurious-RSR rate on the unmodified PMB test set. Full matrix: Backup.

Abstract Description

\[ K = K_{\pi_{\text{pre}}} \land \neg K_{\pi_m} \land \big(K_{\pi_n} \land K_{\pi_{\text{post}}}\big) \land \varphi_{\mathit{Corr}(\pi_m,\pi_n)} \]

where: \(K_{\pi_m}\) represents the reparandum \(K_{\pi_n}\) represents the repair \(K_{\pi_{\text{post}}}\) represents the continuation after the repair

Why Not AMR or DS-TTR

Why SBN rather than AMR (Banarescu et al. 2013) or DS-TTR (Hough and Purver 2012): AMR lacks separate semantic contexts and does not record the order of the replacement; DS-TTR keeps the withdrawn path only in its processing history. SBN separates contexts, and the PMB supplies sentence–SBN pairs.

Noun competitors are WordNet siblings: one step up, then all children

For a noun, the generator goes one step up from the repair concept and takes the parent’s other children as candidates. This is how storm is found for weather, together with cloud, sunset and the rest.

Verbs and adjectives follow the same idea by other routes: VerbNet class-mates for verbs, FrameNet frame-mates and antonyms for adjectives. In each case the candidate fills the same slot with a different value.

Goal: answer “how is storm found?”. Rule: repair_data_gen/wn_candidates.py _cohyponyms. Figure: render_wordnet_siblings.py (reads the sibling list from WordNet). atmospheric_phenomenon.n.01 has 16 children. Next slide answers the follow-up about synonyms.

Synonyms never enter the pool, and storm is a more specific word, not a synonym

  • The pool draws siblings only. WordNet’s near-synonym relations (similar_to clusters, verb groups) are never used, and a candidate that shares a lemma with the repair is dropped. Couch → sofa cannot occur: couch and sofa are lemmas of one synset.
  • In a hand-labelled sample of 150 candidates, annotators judged only 1 to mean the same as the repair.
  • Storm is more specific than weather: an NLI model (Laurer et al. 2024) finds entailment in one direction only (storm ⇒ weather 0.84; weather ⇒ storm 0.005). Moving from a too-specific word to a more general one is a natural repair.

Goal: answer “isn’t a storm a kind of weather?” and “what about synonyms?”. WordNet places storm as a sibling of weather although its gloss reads “a violent weather condition”: the tree and the gloss disagree, which is why the NLI score is shown as a diagnostic. If asked how much NLI filters: nothing. NLI scores are computed and archived for possible later use; the synonym class is empty by construction (pool stage), so a threshold would only remove good candidates. The RP still describes an NLI filter; this is the change since the RP. Why not embedding similarity: it scores hot/cold as similar as sofa/couch, so it cannot separate “interchangeable” from “different values on one dimension”.

Error Categories

Errors are classified as missing configuration, reversed status, wrong link to shared content, reparandum left in accepted content, editing expression represented as content, spurious RSR, or malformed SBN.

Goal: the error categories are fixed before the test outputs are inspected.

Statistics

  • Unit of observation: the PMB source document; all variants of a source are averaged before comparison.
  • Seeds: for each model size and matched seed, paired document-level differences with 95% confidence intervals from 10,000 paired bootstrap samples; within each size, the mean and range of the three differences summarise training variation. A result counts as stable only when all three differences have the same direction.
  • Multiple comparisons: Holm correction within each family of secondary comparisons.
